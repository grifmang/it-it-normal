# The Trump Administration Has Expanded Use of Administrative Warrants for ICE Enforcement Operations

## Background

Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) has authority to issue two types of warrants: administrative warrants and criminal warrants. Administrative warrants are issued by ICE supervisory personnel under authority granted by the Immigration and Nationality Act to arrest individuals for civil immigration violations. These differ from judicial warrants, which are issued by judges or magistrates upon a showing of probable cause and provide Fourth Amendment protections.

## The Distinction Between Warrant Types

**Administrative Warrants:**
- Issued by ICE officials, not judges
- Authorize arrest for immigration violations
- Based on administrative probable cause standards
- Do not require judicial review
- Sufficient for immigration enforcement purposes under federal law

**Judicial Warrants:**
- Issued by judges or magistrates
- Require probable cause determination by neutral arbiter
- Provide full Fourth Amendment protections
- Required for criminal prosecutions
- Generally required for law enforcement to enter homes without consent

## The Current Expansion

The Trump administration has directed ICE to increase enforcement operations as part of its immigration policy priorities. This operational expansion has included increased reliance on administrative warrants as the primary legal mechanism for enforcement actions. While administrative warrants have been used by ICE for decades, the volume and scope of their use has increased significantly since early 2025.

## Legal and Political Response

Democratic officials in multiple jurisdictions have challenged the sufficiency of administrative warrants for certain enforcement actions. Some cities and states have instructed local law enforcement and agencies not to honor ICE detainer requests or facilitate arrests based solely on administrative warrants, arguing that judicial warrants should be required for their cooperation.

Civil liberties organizations have raised concerns that expanded use of administrative warrants circumvents judicial oversight and may lead to enforcement actions that would not meet probable cause standards required for judicial warrants.

The administration and ICE officials maintain that administrative warrants are legally sufficient under federal immigration law and that ICE has clear statutory authority to conduct enforcement operations using these instruments.

## Legal Framework

The legal authority for administrative warrants stems from 8 U.S.C. § 1357, which authorizes immigration officers to issue warrants for arrests of individuals believed to be in violation of immigration law. Federal courts have generally upheld the validity of administrative warrants for immigration enforcement purposes, while also recognizing limitations on their use for certain actions, particularly involuntary home entries.

The distinction has become a central point of tension between federal immigration enforcement and local jurisdictions asserting their own constitutional and policy priorities.
