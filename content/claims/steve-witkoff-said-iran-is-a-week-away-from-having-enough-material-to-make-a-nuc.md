# Steve Witkoff Said Iran Is 'a Week Away' From Having Enough Material to Make a Nuclear Bomb

## The Claim

Steve Witkoff, serving as a U.S. Middle East envoy, stated that Iran is approximately one week away from having sufficient material to construct a nuclear bomb.

## What We Know

### Iran's Uranium Enrichment Program

Iran has developed significant uranium enrichment capabilities in recent years. According to IAEA reporting, Iran has accumulated stockpiles of uranium enriched to 60 percent purity. Weapons-grade uranium typically requires enrichment to approximately 90 percent purity.

The quantity of enriched uranium Iran possesses has grown substantially. The material enriched to 60 percent represents a technical threshold that places Iran closer to weapons capability than lower enrichment levels used for civilian nuclear energy programs.

### Technical Requirements for Nuclear Weapons Material

The process of enriching uranium from 60 percent to 90 percent weapons-grade material involves technical steps that require time and operational capacity. Nuclear weapons experts who reviewed Witkoff's statement expressed skepticism about a one-week timeline for this enrichment process.

Additional factors affect the timeline beyond enrichment alone. These include centrifuge capacity, operational decisions about enrichment priorities, and technical constraints of the enrichment process at higher purity levels.

### Verification Challenges

The IAEA has reported an inability to verify whether Iran has suspended or continued uranium enrichment activities. This verification gap creates uncertainty about real-time assessments of Iran's nuclear program status and capabilities.

The lack of complete monitoring access affects the precision of timeline estimates. Without continuous verification, assessments of how quickly Iran could produce weapons-grade material involve assumptions about unobserved activities.

### Nuclear Weapons Development Beyond Fissile Material

Producing weapons-grade fissile material represents one component of nuclear weapons development. Additional requirements include weaponization design, engineering of triggering mechanisms, miniaturization for delivery systems, and testing or validation of weapon functionality.

These additional steps require technical expertise, infrastructure, and time beyond the production of fissile material itself. The one-week timeline in Witkoff's statement specifically addresses material production rather than the complete process of building a deliverable nuclear weapon.

## Context

The statement occurred during a period of U.S.-Iran diplomatic engagement on nuclear issues in February 2025. Reports characterized these talks as having positive elements, though substantial disagreements remained.

Iran's nuclear program has been a subject of international concern and negotiation since the early 2000s. The 2015 Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action imposed restrictions on Iran's nuclear activities in exchange for sanctions relief. Following U.S. withdrawal from that agreement in 2018 and subsequent Iranian steps away from its commitments, Iran expanded its enrichment activities beyond the agreement's limits.

The current situation reflects this post-agreement period, with Iran having developed capabilities and stockpiles that exceed previous restrictions while international monitoring has become more limited.
