# Claim Analysis: Biden Reversed Felon Deportation Policy

## The Claim

The claim states that Joe Biden reversed a policy of deporting people in the country illegally who have committed felonies.

## What the Evidence Shows

The Biden administration implemented a priority-based immigration enforcement system beginning in early 2021. The interim guidance issued by ICE on February 18, 2021, identified three priority categories for enforcement: national security threats, individuals who recently crossed the border unlawfully, and public safety threats including those engaged in serious criminal conduct.

On September 30, 2021, DHS Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas issued a comprehensive memorandum establishing civil immigration enforcement priorities. This guidance explicitly listed individuals who pose threats to public safety as enforcement priorities, specifically including those convicted of aggravated felonies and other serious criminal offenses.

The enforcement framework represented a shift from previous policies that treated all immigration violations with similar priority levels. The Biden administration directed ICE to focus resources on specific categories rather than pursuing removal of all individuals who could potentially be deported. However, this prioritization maintained convicted felons, particularly those with serious or aggravated felonies, as a top enforcement category.

ICE data from fiscal year 2021 and subsequent years shows that deportations of individuals with criminal convictions continued during the Biden administration, though the total number of deportations and enforcement actions fluctuated based on various factors including resource allocation and policy implementation.

## Context and Interpretation

The claim conflates a change in enforcement prioritization with elimination of felon deportations. While the Biden administration did modify how immigration enforcement resources were allocated, it did not reverse the policy of deporting individuals convicted of felonies. The official guidance documents from both the interim period and the final policy framework explicitly maintain felons as enforcement priorities.

The practical effect of the policy was to direct limited enforcement resources toward individuals deemed to pose the greatest threats to national security and public safety, which included convicted felons, rather than pursuing all immigration violations equally. This represents a methodological change in enforcement strategy rather than a reversal of the policy to deport individuals who have committed felonies.
