evidenceFor:
  - >-
    California has statutory authority under Clean Air Act Section 209(b) to
    establish vehicle emissions standards that differ from federal standards, a
    power unique among states dating to 1967
  - >-
    EPA has historically granted California waivers for vehicle emissions
    standards, including for Advanced Clean Cars II regulations in 2022
  - >-
    Other states can adopt California's standards under Clean Air Act Section
    177, creating a multi-state regulatory framework independent of federal
    baseline standards
evidenceAgainst:
  - >-
    California's authority to set emissions standards is not absolute and
    requires EPA approval through a waiver process, meaning EPA action or
    inaction directly affects California's regulatory power
  - >-
    The claim suggests California gains new authority after EPA policy changes,
    when California has held this authority for decades under existing federal
    law
  - >-
    Previous administrations have revoked California waivers, including the
    Trump administration's 2019 revocation of California's waiver for greenhouse
    gas and zero-emission vehicle standards, demonstrating federal government
    can challenge state authority
  - >-
    California's emissions standards authority exists regardless of whether EPA
    abandons climate policy, as the waiver provision operates independently of
    federal emissions stringency

whatThisMeans:
  - >-
    California does not gain new authority from EPA climate policy changes; it
    has held statutory power to set vehicle emissions standards since 1967,
    subject to EPA waiver approval
  - >-
    The relationship between federal and California emissions standards is
    legally complex, with California's authority dependent on EPA waiver grants
    that can be contested or revoked by federal administrations
  - >-
    If EPA reduces federal emissions standards, California's existing state
    standards could become more stringent than federal baselines, but this
    reflects exercise of existing authority rather than newly created powers
  - >-
    The practical impact depends on whether EPA maintains, revokes, or grants
    California waivers during policy shifts, not on whether California can
    independently create standards without federal involvement
