# Nearly 27% of all homes sold in the first quarter of 2025 were bought by investors

## Claim Details

The claim states that nearly 27% of all homes sold in Q1 2025 were purchased by investors. This statistic relates to ongoing concerns about investor activity in the residential real estate market and its impact on housing affordability and availability for individual homebuyers.

## Evidence Analysis

### Supporting Evidence

According to Redfin data, 26.5% of U.S. homes purchased in Q1 2024 were bought by investors. This figure rounds to approximately 27% and represents about one in four homes sold during that quarter. The data encompasses purchases by both institutional investors and individual real estate investors, including those who purchase properties for rental income or resale.

The investor share of home purchases has remained elevated in recent years compared to historical averages. This trend has generated policy discussions about the impact of investor activity on housing affordability and first-time homebuyer access to the market.

### Contextual Issues

The primary issue with the claim is the temporal reference. The claim cites Q1 2025, but comprehensive quarterly data for Q1 2025 would not typically be compiled, analyzed, and publicly released until several months after the quarter ended on March 31, 2025. The 26.5% figure appears to reference Q1 2024 data, representing a one-year discrepancy between the claim and the available evidence.

Additionally, investor purchase percentages can vary based on how "investor" is defined. Some analyses include only institutional investors or large-scale purchasers, while others include individual investors purchasing single properties. Geographic variations are also significant, with some markets experiencing much higher investor activity than the national average.

## Conclusion

The numerical claim of approximately 27% is supported by available data, but the time period appears to be misstated. The 26.5% figure comes from Q1 2024, not Q1 2025. This represents either a misstatement of the year or a citation of data that would not yet be available for the claimed time period.
