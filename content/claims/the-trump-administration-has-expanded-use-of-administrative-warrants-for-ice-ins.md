# The Trump Administration Has Expanded Use of Administrative Warrants for ICE Instead of Judicial Warrants

## Background

Immigration and Customs Enforcement uses two types of warrants in its enforcement operations: administrative warrants and judicial warrants. Administrative warrants are issued by immigration officials themselves under their civil enforcement authority. Judicial warrants are issued by judges, typically for criminal violations. The distinction reflects whether the underlying matter is civil or criminal in nature.

## The Legal Framework

Under the Immigration and Nationality Act, ICE has authority to arrest individuals for civil immigration violations without obtaining judicial warrants. Administrative warrants are issued pursuant to regulations allowing immigration officers to authorize arrests for immigration violations. This authority has existed for decades across multiple administrations.

Judicial warrants are typically obtained when an individual is charged with criminal violations, such as illegal reentry after deportation or document fraud. These warrants must meet Fourth Amendment probable cause standards and are issued by judges or magistrates.

## Current Practice

ICE enforcement operations rely primarily on administrative warrants because most immigration violations are civil matters rather than criminal offenses. Overstaying a visa or being present without authorization are civil violations. The use of administrative warrants for these situations represents standard practice.

The Trump administration has increased immigration enforcement operations overall. This increase in enforcement activity means more warrants of all types are being issued in absolute numbers. However, the proportion of administrative warrants versus judicial warrants reflects the underlying legal framework rather than a specific policy directive to shift from one type to another.

## Legal Distinctions

Courts have held that administrative warrants do not carry the same Fourth Amendment weight as judicial warrants. Administrative warrants alone may not authorize forced entry into homes without consent. This legal distinction applies regardless of which administration is conducting enforcement.

Some jurisdictions and advocates have raised concerns about administrative warrants being presented in ways that may confuse individuals about their rights. The legal protections afforded by administrative warrants versus judicial warrants differ significantly.

## Context on Enforcement Levels

Enforcement priorities and activity levels have varied across administrations. The Trump administration has emphasized broader immigration enforcement compared to the previous administration, which had implemented narrower enforcement priorities. This difference in enforcement scope affects the total number of warrants issued but does not represent a change in the type of warrant authority ICE possesses or primarily uses.

## Conclusion

The claim that the Trump administration has expanded use of administrative warrants contains elements of truth but lacks important context. ICE has used administrative warrants as its primary tool for civil immigration enforcement for decades. While enforcement operations have increased, resulting in more administrative warrants being issued, this reflects increased enforcement activity rather than a policy shift in warrant type preference. The legal framework governing when ICE uses administrative versus judicial warrants predates the current administration.
