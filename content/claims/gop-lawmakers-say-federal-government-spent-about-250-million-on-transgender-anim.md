# GOP lawmakers say federal government spent about $250 million on transgender animal testing

## Current Status: Mixed

Federal spending records support that approximately $250 million was allocated to animal studies involving sex hormones and biological sex differences, but the characterization of this research as "transgender animal testing" is contested.

## Evidence Supporting the Claim

The $250 million figure cited by GOP lawmakers originates from White Coat Waste Project analysis of federal research spending. Federal agencies, primarily the National Institutes of Health, funded numerous studies examining sex hormones, hormone replacement effects, and biological sex differences in animal models over multiple years.

These research grants covered studies involving hormone administration to animals, examination of sex-based differences in disease progression, and investigations into how sex hormones affect various physiological processes. The compiled spending on such research reached approximately $250 million according to the organization's calculations.

## Evidence Against or Adding Context

The primary dispute concerns categorization rather than the spending amount itself. The term "transgender" refers to human gender identity, a concept that does not apply to animal subjects. Animals in these studies do not have gender identity in the human sense.

The research in question is typically described by scientists as studying sex differences, endocrinology, or hormone effects on physiology. Such studies have standard applications in biomedical research, including understanding how biological sex affects disease progression, how hormones influence cancer development, and how reproductive hormones affect various organ systems.

Studies examining sex differences in animal models serve to understand human health conditions where biological sex plays a role, including cardiovascular disease, autoimmune disorders, and various cancers. Hormone research in animals has been a standard component of biomedical research for decades.

## What This Means

The factual basis of federal spending on animal research involving sex hormones and biological differences is documented, supporting the dollar amount cited. However, the terminology used to describe this research carries interpretive weight.

Whether this spending represents appropriate use of federal research funds depends on assessments of the scientific value and necessity of specific studies. Animal research involving hormones and sex differences serves established purposes in understanding human biology, though individual studies vary in their relevance and design.

The political framing of this spending as "transgender animal testing" differs substantially from how the scientific community describes such research, reflecting broader debates about federal research priorities and spending rather than purely factual disagreement about expenditure amounts.
