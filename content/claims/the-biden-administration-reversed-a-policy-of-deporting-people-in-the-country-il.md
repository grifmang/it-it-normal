# The Biden administration reversed a policy of deporting people in the country illegally who have committed felonies

## Current Status: Unsupported

The claim that the Biden administration reversed a policy of deporting individuals who have committed felonies is not supported by available evidence. While the administration did modify enforcement priorities, felony convictions remained grounds for deportation.

## Evidence Supporting the Claim

The Biden administration did implement changes to immigration enforcement priorities compared to the Trump administration:

- DHS issued new enforcement priority guidelines in February 2021 and September 2021 that narrowed the focus of immigration enforcement activities
- The approach represented a departure from the Trump administration's policy of treating all immigration violations as enforcement priorities

## Evidence Contradicting the Claim

Multiple sources indicate that deportations of individuals with felony convictions continued under Biden administration policies:

- The September 2021 DHS memorandum on enforcement priorities explicitly designated individuals who pose threats to public safety as a top priority, stating that this includes those convicted of felonies, particularly those involving violence or gang-related activity
- The February 2021 interim guidance from Secretary Mayorkas listed as priorities individuals who pose threats to national security, public safety, and border security
- ICE Enforcement and Removal Operations data from fiscal year 2021 shows that the agency continued to deport individuals with criminal convictions
- The enforcement priority framework directed ICE to focus resources on the greatest threats rather than eliminating deportations for any category of individuals

## Timeline of Events

**January 20, 2021**: Biden administration begins and issues initial immigration enforcement memorandum

**February 18, 2021**: DHS Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas issues interim guidance titled "Review of and Interim Revision to Civil Immigration Enforcement and Removal Priorities," which establishes interim enforcement priorities including threats to public safety

**September 30, 2021**: Secretary Mayorkas issues comprehensive guidelines for civil immigration enforcement, explicitly listing public safety threats as priority enforcement targets

**Fiscal Year 2021**: ICE continues removal operations targeting individuals with criminal convictions

## What This Means

The Biden administration's changes to immigration enforcement represented a shift in resource allocation rather than an elimination of deportations for specific categories. The policy change moved from broad enforcement of all immigration violations to prioritizing individuals who posed the greatest threats to public safety, national security, and border security.

Individuals convicted of felonies, particularly violent crimes or crimes threatening national security, remained explicitly listed as enforcement priorities under Biden administration guidance. The policy did not reverse deportations for felony offenders but rather focused limited resources on those deemed to pose the most significant threats.

The distinction between the Trump and Biden approaches lies in the breadth of enforcement: the Trump administration treated all removable individuals as priorities, while the Biden administration directed resources toward specific categories. This represents a change in enforcement strategy rather than a reversal of deportation policy for individuals with felony convictions.
