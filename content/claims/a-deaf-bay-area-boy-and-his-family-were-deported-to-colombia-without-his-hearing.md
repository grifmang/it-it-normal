# A deaf Bay Area boy and his family were deported to Colombia without his hearing aids

## Claim Details

California State Superintendent of Public Instruction Tony Thurmond stated that a deaf student from the Bay Area was deported to Colombia with his family and currently does not have his hearing aids with him. Thurmond publicly called on the Trump administration to return the family to the United States.

## Evidence Supporting the Claim

State Superintendent Tony Thurmond, the elected official overseeing California's public education system, confirmed that a deaf Bay Area student was deported to Colombia and does not have his hearing aids with him. Thurmond made public statements calling on the Trump administration to return the family to the United States. The confirmation came from a state official with direct knowledge of students in California's education system.

## Evidence Against or Providing Context

No sources have disputed the core facts of the claim regarding the deportation or the absence of hearing aids. The claim is supported by statements from a state education official.

## What This Means

The deportation of a deaf minor without essential hearing assistive devices raises questions about protocols for ensuring minors retain necessary medical equipment during immigration enforcement actions. California state education officials have taken the unusual step of publicly opposing a federal deportation action involving a student enrolled in the state's education system. The case illustrates potential conflicts between state-level child welfare concerns and federal immigration enforcement priorities.
