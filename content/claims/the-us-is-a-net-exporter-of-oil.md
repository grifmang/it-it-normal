# The U.S. is a net exporter of oil

## Current Status

The United States has been a net exporter of petroleum products since 2020, according to U.S. Energy Information Administration data. This means the U.S. exports more total petroleum products than it imports when measured in barrels per day.

## Key Context

The characterization of the U.S. as a net oil exporter requires understanding the distinction between crude oil and petroleum products. The U.S. imports crude oil, particularly heavy crude grades, and exports refined products such as gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel. The net exporter status reflects the total balance of all petroleum imports and exports.

According to EIA data, the U.S. petroleum trade includes:

- Crude oil imports from Canada, Mexico, and other countries
- Exports of refined petroleum products to global markets
- Exports of some domestically produced crude oil
- Imports of some refined products

## Trade Patterns

U.S. refineries are configured to process specific types of crude oil, including heavy crude that is not produced domestically in sufficient quantities. This creates ongoing demand for crude imports even as the U.S. produces record volumes of light crude oil from shale formations.

The U.S. refining sector processes both domestic and imported crude oil into products that serve domestic markets and export markets. This results in the U.S. simultaneously importing crude and exporting refined products.

## Production Levels

The U.S. is the world's largest producer of crude oil and natural gas liquids. Domestic production increased significantly during the 2010s due to advances in hydraulic fracturing and horizontal drilling technologies that unlocked shale oil resources.

## Market Integration

Net exporter status does not mean the U.S. is isolated from global oil markets. Crude oil and petroleum products are globally traded commodities, and U.S. prices remain influenced by international supply and demand dynamics. Events affecting global oil supply, such as production disruptions or geopolitical conflicts, continue to affect U.S. petroleum markets.

## Measurement Considerations

Net export calculations can vary month-to-month based on seasonal demand patterns, refinery maintenance schedules, and market conditions. Annual averages provide a more stable measure of the U.S. trade position in petroleum products.
