# New York ranks number one in the nation for individual tax burden

## Background

State tax burden comparisons attempt to measure the total amount of state and local taxes paid relative to income or economic activity. These rankings are produced by various organizations using different methodologies, including measuring taxes as a percentage of state income, per capita tax collections, or effective tax rates across income levels.

New York has long been recognized as a high-tax state, with significant income taxes, property taxes, and sales taxes at both state and local levels. New York City imposes additional local income taxes that compound the burden for city residents.

## Evidence Summary

### Supporting Evidence

New York consistently appears at or near the top of state tax burden rankings. The state imposes a progressive income tax with rates reaching 10.9 percent for the highest earners, among the highest state income tax rates nationally. New York City residents face an additional local income tax ranging from 3.078 to 3.876 percent, creating combined state and local income tax rates that exceed those in nearly all other jurisdictions.

Property taxes in New York are also substantial, particularly in suburban counties around New York City and in certain upstate metropolitan areas. When combined with sales taxes and other levies, the total state and local tax burden places New York consistently in the top two positions nationally.

### Contextual Factors

The precise ranking depends significantly on methodology. Some analyses measure taxes paid by state residents regardless of where the economic activity occurs, while others measure all taxes collected within a state's borders. These different approaches can produce different results.

Connecticut frequently rivals New York for the highest tax burden position, and in some recent years has ranked first depending on the analysis method used. The competition between these two states for the top position means that while New York is definitively among the highest-taxed states, claiming the exact number one spot requires specifying the particular metric and time period.

Tax burdens also vary considerably within New York. New York City residents face higher combined burdens than residents of upstate areas due to city income taxes, while property tax rates vary significantly by county and municipality.

## Analysis

The claim that New York ranks number one for individual tax burden is substantially accurate but lacks the precision needed to be fully verified. New York is consistently ranked either first or second among all states for tax burden, depending on the specific methodology and year examined. For practical purposes, stating New York has the highest or among the highest tax burdens would be more defensible than claiming definitively that it ranks exactly number one in all contexts.

The distinction between first and second place, when the difference is often minimal, may be less meaningful than the broader reality that New York taxpayers face substantially higher burdens than the vast majority of other states. The claim captures an important truth about New York's tax environment even if it overstates the certainty of the exact ranking.
