evidenceFor:
  - >-
    The Committee for Responsible Federal Budget documented that the average
    interest rate on federal debt exceeded nominal GDP growth in 2024, marking
    the first time this occurred since the early 2000s
  - >-
    The Congressional Budget Office projects federal debt held by the public
    will increase from 99% of GDP in 2024 to 118% by 2035
  - >-
    Net interest costs on federal debt reached approximately $892 billion in
    fiscal year 2024, representing a significant increase from prior years
  - >-
    When interest rates exceed GDP growth, the debt-to-GDP ratio tends to
    increase even with a balanced primary budget, creating adverse debt dynamics
evidenceAgainst:
  - >-
    The term 'debt spiral' implies an imminent, self-reinforcing crisis, but
    economists note that debt sustainability depends on multiple factors
    including primary deficits, inflation, and market confidence, not solely the
    interest rate-GDP growth differential
  - >-
    The United States retains strong credit ratings and continued market access
    to borrow at relatively stable rates, indicating investor confidence remains
    intact
  - >-
    Historical instances of interest rates exceeding GDP growth have not
    automatically resulted in fiscal crises in countries with reserve currencies
    and deep capital markets
  - >-
    The Congressional Budget Office baseline projections, while showing
    increasing debt levels, do not project an imminent debt crisis or loss of
    market access in the next decade

whatThisMeans:
  - >-
    The relationship between interest rates and GDP growth is a key metric for
    debt sustainability because when interest rates exceed growth rates, debt
    grows faster relative to the economy's capacity to service it
  - >-
    This fiscal development increases pressure on the federal budget as interest
    payments consume a larger share of government revenue, potentially crowding
    out other spending priorities or requiring higher taxes or additional
    borrowing
  - >-
    While this metric represents a concerning fiscal trend, the characterization
    as an imminent 'debt spiral' overstates the certainty and timing of
    potential crisis, as debt dynamics depend on multiple factors including
    fiscal policy choices, inflation rates, and continued market confidence
  - >-
    Countries with reserve currencies and deep capital markets historically have
    more flexibility to manage elevated debt levels than the simple interest
    rate-GDP comparison would suggest, though this does not eliminate fiscal
    risks
