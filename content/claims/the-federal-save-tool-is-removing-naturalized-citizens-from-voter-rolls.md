# The federal SAVE tool is removing naturalized citizens from voter rolls

## Context

The Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) program is a Department of Homeland Security service that allows government agencies to verify immigration status. Some states have used SAVE to help identify noncitizens who may be registered to vote. Questions have arisen about whether this practice inappropriately targets naturalized U.S. citizens.

## The Evidence

### How SAVE Works

The SAVE system was created to verify immigration status for benefits eligibility determinations. When an agency queries the system with an individual's information, SAVE returns data about that person's immigration status based on DHS records. The system was not originally designed for voter registration verification.

### State Use for Voter Roll Maintenance

State election officials in multiple jurisdictions have used SAVE data to cross-check voter registration lists. When SAVE indicates a registered voter may be a noncitizen, states typically send notices to those individuals requesting proof of citizenship.

### Impact on Naturalized Citizens

The SAVE system primarily tracks immigration status and visa information. When a lawful permanent resident naturalizes as a U.S. citizen, this change may not be immediately reflected in SAVE queries. As a result, naturalized citizens can be flagged as noncitizens when their records are checked against the SAVE database.

States that have conducted SAVE-assisted voter roll reviews have identified both actual noncitizens and naturalized citizens. Flagged individuals generally receive notice and an opportunity to provide documentation of their citizenship status before any removal occurs.

### Distribution of Authority

The SAVE system provides information but does not make determinations about voter eligibility or execute removals from voter rolls. State election officials receive SAVE data and then follow state-specific procedures to determine whether to challenge or remove a voter's registration.

## Key Distinctions

The claim that "the federal SAVE tool is removing naturalized citizens from voter rolls" contains elements of truth but presents the process inaccurately. The SAVE tool provides data that states use in voter roll maintenance. When states act on this data, some naturalized citizens are flagged and may be removed if they do not respond to verification requests. However, the federal tool itself does not remove voters, and procedural protections typically exist to prevent improper removals.
