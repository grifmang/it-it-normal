  Federal law requires photo identification to purchase alcohol for individuals

  document requirement at all for in-person voting.

evidenceFor:
  - >-
    Federal regulations require retailers to check photo identification for
    alcohol purchases when a customer appears to be under 27 years of age,
    creating a nationwide standard
  - >-
    As of 2024, 14 states allow voters to cast regular ballots without
    presenting any identification documents at the polling place
  - >-
    States including California, Illinois, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts,
    Minnesota, Nevada, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Pennsylvania, Vermont,
    and Wyoming do not require identification documents for in-person voting
  - >-
    Some states that request ID allow voters to sign an affidavit or have poll
    workers vouch for their identity rather than presenting documents
evidenceAgainst:
  - >-
    36 states have laws requiring or requesting voters to show some form of
    identification at the polls as of 2024
  - >-
    Seven states have strict photo ID requirements where voters cannot cast a
    regular ballot without presenting photo identification
  - >-
    Alcohol ID requirements only apply to individuals who appear under a certain
    age, while voter ID laws apply to all voters regardless of appearance
  - >-
    Many states accept multiple forms of identification for voting, including
    non-photo documents such as utility bills, bank statements, or government
    checks
  - >-
    Voters who lack ID in many states can still vote through provisional ballots
    or by providing identification after Election Day

whatThisMeans:
  - >-
    The claim oversimplifies two different regulatory systems: federal alcohol
    sales regulations create uniform requirements nationwide, while voting
    requirements are determined by individual states under their constitutional
    authority to administer elections
  - >-
    The comparison lacks context because alcohol ID checks are age-based and
    applied selectively, while voter ID requirements apply to all voters in
    states where they exist
  - >-
    States without voter ID document requirements typically use other
    verification methods, such as signature matching against voter registration
    records or requiring voters to state their name and address
  - >-
    The variation in state voting requirements means the claim is accurate for
    some states but not others, making blanket statements about voting ID
    requirements nationally misleading
