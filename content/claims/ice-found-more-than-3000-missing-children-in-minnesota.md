# ICE Found More Than 3,000 Missing Children in Minnesota

## Claim Classification: Unsupported

The claim that U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement found more than 3,000 missing children in Minnesota lacks supporting evidence in available records. The claim appears to mischaracterize data regarding unaccompanied minors in the federal tracking system.

## Background

The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office of Refugee Resettlement maintains responsibility for unaccompanied children who cross the border without parents or legal guardians. After processing, these children are released to vetted sponsors, typically family members or other adults in the United States. HHS conducts follow-up calls after placement to check on child welfare, but these calls are voluntary.

## Evidence Assessment

### Supporting Context

HHS does track unaccompanied minors through its post-release services program. The department attempts to contact sponsors at 30-day intervals after placement. Some sponsors do not respond to these calls. Minnesota has been a destination state for some unaccompanied minors placed with sponsors.

### Key Issues With The Claim

No publicly available ICE operational report, press release, or official statement documents an operation in which the agency found or located 3,000 missing children in Minnesota. ICE's enforcement operations focus on immigration law violations and removal proceedings, not locating children under HHS supervision.

The 3,000 figure appears to conflate children whose sponsors did not respond to voluntary follow-up calls with children who were missing and then physically located by ICE. These are distinct categories. A sponsor not answering a phone call does not mean the child is missing from their placement, nor does it mean ICE conducted an operation to find them.

HHS, not ICE, maintains primary jurisdiction over tracking unaccompanied minors after release to sponsors. Any large-scale operation to locate children would typically involve HHS coordination and would be documented in official reporting.

## Official Tracking Systems

The HHS Office of Refugee Resettlement maintains data on unaccompanied children, including placement information and follow-up call outcomes. The Department of Homeland Security Office of Inspector General has conducted reviews of how federal agencies track these children. These systems distinguish between children whose location is unknown and those whose sponsors simply did not respond to follow-up contact attempts.

## Context on "Missing" Terminology

In immigration system tracking, "missing" can refer to multiple scenarios: children who have left sponsor care without notification, sponsors who cannot be reached by phone, children who have moved, or sponsors who decline to participate in voluntary follow-up. These differ significantly from children who are missing in a law enforcement sense and subsequently recovered through investigative operations.

## What We Know With Certainty

- HHS tracks unaccompanied minors after release to sponsors through follow-up calls
- Some sponsors do not respond to these voluntary calls
- Minnesota has received unaccompanied minors through the federal placement system
- No verified ICE operational data confirms finding 3,000 missing children in Minnesota

## What Remains Unclear

The specific origin of the 3,000 figure has not been documented in publicly available federal records. If such an operation occurred, standard government transparency practices would typically result in official documentation, press releases, or congressional notification, none of which appear in the public record for this specific claim.
